## Story

The question is clear and the order is right. Three links in the argument are missing:

- **No demand estimates appear anywhere.** The deck goes from instruments (slide 8) to supply (slide 9) without a coefficient, standard error or elasticity.
- **The negative costs are shown but not explained.** Under linear price, logit elasticity is proportional to price, so a $0.52 roll gets about −1.0 while large packs get about −5.8. Under log price every product gets about −5.2. That one fact is the mechanism, and it is absent.
- **Slides 11 and 12 are not connected.** The scenarios with the largest merger effects (linear price, high nesting) are the ones where 80–100% of Scott single-roll volume has negative cost. The talk should say this in one sentence.

A likely reason for that last link is that Kimberly-Clark's price rise scales with Scott's implied markup, the same markup that is too large. I have not computed Kimberly-Clark-to-Scott diversion; it is a closed-form number from saved shares and would make the point concrete.
